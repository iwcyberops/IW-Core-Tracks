# Chapter 05: Become an Elusive Object of Desire
> **The Law of Covetousness**  
> *"Absence and presence have primal psychological effects. Too much presence suffocates and breeds contempt; strategic absence sparks obsession and desire. Humans continually crave what they cannot easily have. To become an object of desire, you must master the art of mystery, create rivalries, and dangle the forbidden."*

---

## 📌 Executive Summary
Human beings are rarely content with what they already possess. The moment we obtain something, our mind drifts toward what is missing, distant, or forbidden. This psychological phenomenon is known as the **"Grass-is-Always-Greener" syndrome**.

Most people destroy their own value by being **too available, too transparent, and overly eager to please**. When you expose everything about yourself, you leave zero room for other people's imagination.

To command attention and desire in business, marketing, and personal relationships, you must **reverse your perspective**: stop focusing on what *you* want, and start triggering the repressed fantasies, rivalries, and curiosity of *others*.

## 👒 Part 1: The Master Case Study: Coco Chanel

* **The Background:** Gabrielle "Coco" Chanel grew up in extreme poverty in an austere French convent orphanage after her mother died and her father abandoned her. She survived by reading romantic novels and dreaming of a luxurious, independent life.
* **The Breakthrough:** 
  * While living among elite courtesans at a country estate, Chanel noticed women were physically imprisoned by heavy corsets, oversized gowns, and complex hats.
  * She began wearing men's clothes—open-collar shirts, tweed jackets, jodhpurs, and simple straw boater hats.
  * **The Psychological Shift:** Instead of copying the wealthy elite, she made them envy her freedom and androgynous style.
* **The Seduction Strategies She Used:**
  1. **Aura of Mystery:** She never revealed her impoverished childhood, invented contradictory stories about her past, rarely gave interviews, and disappeared from the public eye for long periods.
  2. **The Transgressive Edge:** She used cheap, comfortable jersey fabric (previously used only for men's underwear) to make high-end luxury dresses, blurring gender lines and redefining modern female freedom.
  3. **The Launch of Chanel No. 5:**
     * Instead of traditional floral scents, she engineered an abstract, synthetic bouquet that smelled unique on every woman.
     * She gave it a cold, scientific name (*Chanel No. 5*) and packaged it in a sleek, minimalist bottle.
     * **Subliminal Rollout:** She sprayed the perfume in her Paris boutique without telling customers what it was, and gave unlabelled bottles to high-society influencers, creating an unstoppable viral word-of-mouth frenzy.
  4. **Embracing Imitation:** Instead of fighting counterfeiters, she allowed copycats to reproduce her designs, knowing that ubiquity made the original *Chanel* brand the supreme object of global desire.
* **The Lesson:** Chanel never begged for attention. She created an irresistible mystique and let people's natural covetousness do the work.

### 1. The Law of Induction (The Power of Contrast)
* The human brain perceives things in opposites. When you see bright red, your eyes automatically create a green contrast around it.
* In psychology, **whenever something is forbidden or declared taboo, the brain instantly fixates on it**. (e.g., Victorian bans on sexuality gave birth to the modern pornography industry).

### 2. Evolutionary Restlessness
* Early humans could never afford to be completely satisfied. If a tribe became complacent, they starved or were attacked by predators. We are genetically wired to search for what is missing.

### 3. The Power of Imagination (fMRI Science)
* Brain scans reveal that when we *imagine* an exciting vacation, romance, or luxury item, our brain releases nearly the same dopamine levels as experiencing it in reality.
* Reality always has flaws and limits, but our imagination is infinite. Therefore, **the fantasy of an object is always more intoxicating than owning it**.

## 🏹 Part 3: Three Strategies to Stimulate Desire

```
[ STRATEGY 1: STRATEGIC WITHDRAWAL ] ---> [ STRATEGY 2: RIVALRIES OF DESIRE ] ---> [ STRATEGY 3: INDUCTION & TABOO ]
(Create scarcity & absence)              (Show that others want you)              (Dangle the forbidden & secret)
```

### 1. Know How and When to Withdraw (Scarcity & Absence)
* **A Touch of Coldness:** When you are too friendly and available, people take you for granted. Projecting that you can comfortably live without them instantly elevates your value.
* **Cultivate Blankness:** Do not overshare your opinions, personal history, or routines. Let your presence be a blank screen upon which others project their fantasies (as movie stars and pop icons do).
* **Physical Absence (The Michael Jackson Rule):** 
  * Michael Jackson purposely spaced out his albums by years and strictly limited public interviews. This created massive hunger for his return.
  * **Rule:** Once you have captured someone’s interest, step back. Create an empty space in their mind so they spend time thinking about you.

### 2. Create Rivalries of Desire (Social Proof & Triangulation)
* **Desire is Mimetic:** We do not choose our desires independently; we desire what **other people** desire.
* **How to Apply It:**
  * **In Negotiation:** Always subtly indicate that other third parties are competing for your skills, services, or products. A bidding war instantly multiplies your perceived worth.
  * **In Personal Relationships:** Never brag, but let it be known through third parties that you have options and are respected by others.
  * **In Product Launches:** Highlight waitlists, sold-out stock, and public demand rather than pushing aggressive discounts.

### 3. Use Induction (Associate with the Forbidden and the New)
* **The Transgressive Pull:** People are exhausted by modern rules, political correctness, and micromanagement. They crave what feels bold, unconventional, and rule-breaking.
* **The Voyeurism Angle:** Humans have an intense desire to peek behind closed doors. Frame your knowledge or products as "insider secrets" or "unfiltered truths" that few people have access to.
* **The Shortcut Fantasy:** Dangle the prospect of solving a difficult problem faster or achieving greatness through a unique methodology.

* **The Arthur Schopenhauer Rule:** Treat your availability with discipline:
  > *"It is advisable to let everyone feel now and then that you could very well dispense with their company... they will value your friendship all the more."*

## 📋 Actionable Checklist: The Law of Covetousness
- [ ] **Practice Strategic Silence:** Stop instantly replying to non-urgent messages; let some time elapse.
- [ ] **Limit Self-Disclosure:** Keep 30% of your thoughts, plans, and background private.
- [ ] **Create Scarcity:** Never let clients or partners feel they have 100% guaranteed, unlimited access to you.
- [ ] **Harness Third-Party Proof:** Let others speak of your accomplishments rather than boasting yourself.
- [ ] **Ground Yourself in Reality:** When you feel the itch to abandon a project or relationship for an "exciting new dream," remind yourself that all grass looks greener from far away.
