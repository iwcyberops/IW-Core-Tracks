# Law 16: Use Absence to Increase Respect and Honor

> **Core Rule:** The more you are seen and heard from, the cheaper you become. Too much availability makes people take you for granted. If you are already established in a group, company, or relationship, temporarily withdrawing yourself will create mystery, stop contempt, and make people talk about you with renewed respect. Learn when to leave. Create value through scarcity.

---

## 📌 Executive Summary (The Plain Truth)

Everything in this world is governed by the laws of supply and demand: **what is common is cheap; what is rare is precious.**

Most people make the mistake of being **too available**:
- They answer every message within five seconds.
- They attend every casual meeting and water-cooler gossip session.
- They stay too long at parties and overstay their welcome in leadership positions.

When you are always around, people notice your small flaws, your quirks, and your bad moods. The mystery dies. You become an everyday habit, like the wallpaper.

**The Power Move:**  
First, show up consistently until people recognize your value. Then, **vanish strategically**. 
When you step back:
1. People suddenly feel the vacuum and discomfort of your absence.
2. Their imagination begins to clean away your flaws and blow your good qualities up to heroic proportions.
3. They stop complaining and start asking: *"Where are they? When are they coming back?"*

## 📜 Complete Historical Lessons (Broken Down Into Plain Logic)

### Lesson 1: The Judge Who Staged His Own Retirement (Deioces and the Medes)

* **The Chaos (8th Century B.C.):** The Medes (in northwestern Iran) had successfully thrown off the brutal rule of the Assyrian Empire. Determined to never live under a tyrant again, they refused to appoint a king. But without a central authority, the nation descended into lawlessness: villages fought against villages, crime ran rampant, and the courts were corrupt.
* **The Rise:** In one small village lived a fair-minded man named **Deioces**. He began resolving local neighborhood disputes with absolute honesty and impartiality. 
  - His fame spread across the entire region.
  - Soon, citizens from every province traveled to Deioces to settle their legal battles. He became the sole pillar of justice in the land.
* **The Strategic Strike (The Disappearance):** At the absolute peak of his fame and influence, Deioces suddenly announced: *"I have had enough. I am spending all my time fixing other people's problems and neglecting my own life. I will hear no more cases."* He closed his doors and retired.
* **The Result:** The entire country collapsed into instant, terrifying chaos. Crime exploded, robberies skyrocketed, and citizens lived in mortal fear. 
  - Tribal leaders called an emergency council: *"We cannot survive like this. We must appoint a king to bring order, or we will lose our homes."*
  - Everyone agreed on one name: **Deioces**.
* **The Crown on His Terms:** When the delegation arrived at his house begging him to rule, Deioces agreed—but only under his own strict conditions:
  - An enormous palace was built for him, surrounded by fortress walls.
  - He commanded an elite royal bodyguard.
  - **Total Isolation:** No ordinary citizen was allowed into his presence. All communications were handled through written messengers. Even court nobles could only see him once a week by special royal permission.
* **The Immortality:** Deioces ruled for 53 years. Because nobody saw him wandering through the dusty streets like a common mortal, his legend grew into divine status. The people worshipped him not as a man, but as a god.

> **Lesson:** When you are indispensable, your withdrawal creates panic. Let people experience a taste of life without you, and they will come back on their knees, begging you to take total control on your own terms.

### Lesson 2: The Troubadour’s Foolish Game (Guillaume de Balaun)

* **The Setup:** In medieval France, the poet and knight Sir Guillaume de Balaun was deeply in love with a noblewoman named Lady Guillelma. Their romance was peaceful and mutual.
* **The Bad Idea:** Guillaume had a friend whose relationship had survived a violent quarrel, and his friend told him: *"There is no greater, sweeter ecstasy on earth than the love that follows a painful reconciliation."* Guillaume became obsessed with experiencing that feeling.
* **The False Departure:** Guillaume pretended to be furious with Guillelma, stopped sending poems, packed his bags, and left her castle.
  - **Phase 1 (Success):** Guillelma was devastated. His sudden absence inflamed her love. She sent messengers and love letters begging him to return.
  - **Phase 2 (The Overplay):** Instead of accepting the win, Guillaume wanted more drama. When she sent messengers, he turned them away with insults. When she stopped sending messengers out of exhaustion, Guillaume realized he had pushed too far. He rode back to her castle in fancy armor to "reconcile."
* **The Backfire:** When Guillaume arrived, Guillelma was so hurt and disgusted by his cruel, erratic games that she ordered her guards to chase him over the drawbridge. 
  - Over the next year, Guillaume experienced the true, agonizing pain of real absence. He cried in his bedroom, wrote desperate poems pleading for mercy, and was finally forced to pull off his own pinky fingernail and send it to her in a box as a bloody token of penance before she allowed him back.

> **Lesson:** Absence is a powerful medicine, but an overdose is fatal. If you withdraw for too long, or act with gratuitous cruelty, the other person’s longing will harden into bitter hatred or complete emotional detachment.

### Lesson 3: Knowing When to Step Off the Stage (Charles V & Greta Garbo)

* **Emperor Charles V (1557):** The King of Spain and Holy Roman Emperor ruled an empire that covered Europe and the Americas. At the absolute summit of his power, he did what no emperor had ever done: he abdicated his throne and quietly retired to the remote monastery of Yuste.
  - The entire civilized world was stunned. People who had feared and cursed him as an aggressive warlord suddenly viewed him with awe, calling him a saint and a philosopher-king. His sudden exit preserved his majesty forever.
* **Greta Garbo (1941):** The world’s most celebrated Hollywood actress was in her mid-thirties when she decided to walk away from cinema completely.
  - She gave no press conferences, attended no red carpets, and lived in quiet seclusion.
  - While other movie stars aged in public, lost their beauty, and took bad movie roles for cash, Garbo remained forever young, fascinating, and legendary in the minds of the public.

### Lesson 4: The Cock and the Snow Goose (Ancient Chinese Parable)

* A rooster (*the cock*) complained to his master: *"I wake up early every single morning, I crow to announce the dawn, I protect the hens, and I share food. Yet every Sunday, you kill one of us for dinner. But the wild snow goose does nothing for you, flies thousands of miles away, and eats your grain—yet you treat it like a precious, noble bird!"*
* The master replied: *"You are right next to my kitchen door every second of the day; your presence is common. The snow goose is rare and comes from far away. Humans value what is scarce, not what is standing right under their feet."*

### 1. The Art of Leaving the Room Early
* Never be the last person to leave a business dinner, a client meeting, or a party.
* Exit while the conversation is still at its peak. State a clean, polite reason: *"I have an early operational review tomorrow; wonderful seeing everyone."*
* When you leave while people are still laughing and engaged, they will remember your presence with warmth and wish you had stayed longer. If you hang around until everyone is tired, you leave an impression of exhaustion.

### 2. The Communication Delay (The 1-Hour Buffer)
* Stop responding to every non-urgent email, Slack ping, or WhatsApp message within 30 seconds.
* An immediate reply signals to others: *"I have nothing else important to do with my life."*
* Introduce a calibrated delay: respond after 45–90 minutes with a crisp, concise, high-value answer. You instantly appear busy, focused, and in high demand.

### 3. Supply Restriction in Consulting & Freelancing
* Follow art dealer Joseph Duveen’s model: never dump all your availability or inventory on a prospective client.
* **The Script:** *"We only onboard two enterprise clients per quarter to ensure our engineering bandwidth remains elite. We currently have one slot open for next month."*
* Scarcity triggers instant urgency. If you tell a client: *"I can start tomorrow and work 60 hours a week,"* they will immediately negotiate your rates down.

## 🌐 Modern Workplace & Digital Scenarios

```
                              THE SLACK AVAILABILITY TRAP
             THE 24/7 SLACKER                            THE STRATEGIC SPECIALIST
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐     ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Online status always bright green   │     │ Sets specific "Focus Time" blocks   │
  │ Drops into every random channel     │     │ Silent on non-critical threads      │
  │ Perceived as an entry-level worker  │     │ Solves massive blockers when called │
  │ Low compensation, easily replaced   │     │ Highly paid, untouchable status     │
  └─────────────────────────────────────┘     └─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### In Corporate Politics: The "Over-Communicator" Trap
* An ambitious product manager sends three daily updates, posts in every public company Slack channel, and chimes in on every executive thread.
* **The Reality:** The executive team gets exhausted seeing their face. Their input loses weight because it is everywhere.
* **The Better Move:** The PM posts **one high-impact weekly brief** summarizing milestones, data, and outcomes. Because their communication is rare, the CEO reads every single word.

### In Social Media & Creator Economy
* Creators who post 10 mediocre stories a day burn out their audience’s attention span within six months. The algorithm devalues them, and engagement collapses.
* The most influential voices (e.g., Frank Ocean, top tech researchers, elite essayists) go silent for months. When they finally drop a single project or paper, it breaks the internet because scarcity has turned their output into an event.

### In Cybersecurity: Ephemeral Infrastructure
* Modern high-security environments use **Ephemeral, Short-Lived Systems**.
* Servers and containers do not stay live permanently; they spin up to process a specific workload and terminate within minutes. Attackers have zero permanent surface area to maintain persistent hooks because the target is literally absent 90% of the time.

## 🛡️ The Reversal: When Does Absence Destroy You?

Is there a time when withdrawing will wipe you out? **Yes: when you have not yet established your value.**

### 1. The Trap of Early Withdrawal
* If you start a new job, launch a brand, or enter a new relationship and immediately try to "act scarce" or play hard-to-get, **it will fail completely**.
* People do not miss what they do not know or love. In the beginning, you must be **omnipresent**: show up early, deliver consistently, and make your presence comforting and familiar. Only after you have captured their loyalty do you turn on the scarcity engine.

### 2. Absence in Times of Crisis (The Coward's Exit)
* If your company or team is in the middle of an emergency (a security breach, a financial crisis, an operational fire), **never withdraw**.
* Disappearing during a crisis makes you look like a coward or an incompetent deserter. Step to the front lines, help extinguish the fire, and withdraw only after the victory is secure.
