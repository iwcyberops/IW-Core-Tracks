# Law 34: Be Royal in Your Own Fashion: Act Like a King to Be Treated Like One

> **Core Rule:** The way you carry yourself determines how the world treats you. In the long run, appearing vulgar, apologetic, or common will make people disrespect you. A king respects himself and inspires that same reverence in others. By acting with quiet confidence, self-dignity, and an unshakeable belief in your own value, you make yourself appear destined for greatness.

---

## 📌 Executive Summary (The Plain Truth)

The world accepts you at your own valuation:
- If you slouch, apologize for taking up space, and beg for small scraps, people will treat you like a beggar.
- If you act like an equal to the leaders in the room, maintain an aura of quiet dignity, and set a high price on your time, people will assume you are worth a king's ransom.

Many talented people make the mistake of **trying too hard to appear "humble and common."** They think modesty makes them likable. 

**The Psychological Reality:**  
People do not want their leaders to look like ordinary folks. They crave figures of distinction, poise, and unshakeable self-worth. If you do not respect your own stature, nobody else will do it for you. 

**The Strategy of the Crown:** Believe so firmly in your own value that it radiates outward. Do not wait for someone to crown you; the greatest leaders crown themselves through their posture, their high standards, and their refusal to grovel.

```
   │                 ▼                 │        │                 ▼                 │
   │ Sparks hatred, revolt & execution │        │ Inspires awe, trust & respect     │
   └───────────────────────────────────┘        └───────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Arrogance is noisy and rude:** It comes from people who feel small inside and try to prove their importance by humiliating others. This leads to revolution (e.g., King Charles I of England losing his head).
- **Dignity is quiet and composed:** It comes from within. You do not shout; you do not panic; you do not beg. You treat everyone with respect, but you never bow, scrape, or demean yourself for money or approval.

## 📜 Complete Historical Lessons (Broken Down Into Plain Logic)

### Lesson 1: The King Who Lost His Crown to an Umbrella (Louis-Philippe of France)

* **The Setup (1830):** Following a revolution against Charles X, French authorities gave the crown to Louis-Philippe, the Duke of Orleans.
* **The "Bourgeois King":** Louis-Philippe decided to be a "modern" ruler who rejected royal ceremony:
  - He refused to wear royal robes or carry a scepter.
  - He walked the muddy streets of Paris carrying an ordinary gray hat and an umbrella, shaking hands with common businessmen like a store clerk.
  - When he invited banking titan James Rothschild to his palace, he treated him as an equal and talked exclusively about money and personal investments.
* **The Backfire:** 
  - Instead of winning love, the French people grew disgusted. The working class saw that the "humble" king was actually amassing an enormous private fortune while they starved.
  - The aristocrats despised his lack of majesty.
  - Even the bankers lost all respect for him: one day, James Rothschild publicly berated the King in front of everyone for arriving late to a train departure. The banker treated the King like a subordinate.
* **The Humiliating Fall (1848):** When riots erupted in Paris, Louis-Philippe caved in immediately. He abdicated the throne, shaved his face, disguised himself in civilian clothes, and fled in a common cab to England. 
* **The Immediate Replacement:** The French people promptly elected Napoleon’s nephew as president—because they were starved for **majesty, charisma, and imperial grandeur**.

> **Lesson:** If you strip away your own dignity to seem relatable, you do not gain friendship; you gain contempt. People want leaders who project grandeur, not glorified accountants carrying umbrellas.

### Lesson 2: The Son of a Weaver Who Demanded an Ocean (Christopher Columbus)

* **The Reality:** Christopher Columbus was the son of a poor wool weaver and cheese vendor from Genoa. He was not an aristocrat, possessed mediocre navigation skills, and was heavily in debt.
* **The Manufactured Nobility:** Columbus firmly believed destiny had chosen him for greatness:
  - He created a fabricated backstory claiming descent from Roman generals and Italian counts.
  - He carried himself with the calm, stately posture of a grand lord.
* **The Outrageous Pitch (1484–1492):** When petitioning King Joao II of Portugal, and later Queen Isabella of Spain, for three ships to sail west, he did not beg like a poor sailor. He made royal demands:
  - The hereditary title of **"Grand Admiral of the Oceanic Sea."**
  - The office of Viceroy and Governor-General over all discovered lands.
  - 10% of all revenues, gold, and spices from trade routes forever.
* **The Psychological Payoff:** 
  - King Joao and Queen Isabella were initially shocked by the demands, but they **treated him with respect**.
  - They reasoned: *"A man who asks for such an astronomical prize cannot be an ordinary beggar. He must possess divine certainty."*
  - In 1492, Queen Isabella gave him the ships, the funding, and signed the royal contract granting him the titles. Columbus raised his status from penniless merchant to an immortal figure of world history purely by acting like a king before he had discovered a single island.

> **Lesson:** Set your own price. If you ask for little, the world will give you less. Ask for the moon with absolute, quiet dignity, and people will assume you are worth it.

### Lesson 3: The Prince Who Danced Away His Crown (Hippocleides)

* **The Contest (Ancient Greece):** Cleisthenes, the wealthy master of Sicyon, held an Olympic-style competition to find the finest man in Greece to marry his daughter Agarista. Suitors from all city-states lived in his palace for a year, tested on manners, courage, and intellect.
* **The Front-Runner:** Hippocleides of Athens was by far the favorite. He was handsome, athletic, witty, and Cleisthenes loved his company.
* **The Fatal Loss of Dignity:** At the final banquet where the marriage was to be announced, Hippocleides drank too much wine:
  - He asked the flute player for music and began dancing wild, drunken steps.
  - Cleisthenes watched with growing disgust.
  - Hippocleides then ordered a table brought in, climbed on top, danced Attic dances, and finally **stood on his head, kicking his bare legs in the air**.
* **The Sentence:** Cleisthenes stood up in cold fury: *"Son of Tisander, you have just danced away your marriage!"* Hippocleides was dismissed in disgrace, and the princess was given to another suitor.

> **Lesson:** A single lapse into drunken, clownish vulgarity will instantly wipe out a year of calculated excellence. Never drop your dignity for temporary applause or a cheap laugh.

### Lesson 4: The Unshakeable Stature (Haile Selassie at the League of Nations, 1936)

* When Italian Fascists invaded Ethiopia with poison gas and tanks in 1936, Emperor Haile Selassie was forced into exile.
* He appeared before the League of Nations in Geneva to plead his country’s case.
* **The Harassment:** As he took the podium, Italian journalists in the press gallery began screaming vulgar insults, blowing whistles, and stomping their feet to humiliate him.
* **The Regal Response:** Selassie did not scream back, look down, or show anger. 
  - He stood completely still at the podium, staring ahead with icy, imperial serenity.
  - He waited patiently in absolute silence until security cleared the hecklers.
  - He then delivered his speech in his native Amharic with majestic, slow dignity.
* **The Impact:** The scene was broadcast globally. The Italian fascists looked like screaming hooligans; Selassie looked like an immortal, tragic monarch. He won the moral respect of the entire world, and years later returned to liberate his throne.

### 1. The Gift of Equality (Pietro Aretino’s Method)
* When Renaissance satirist Pietro Aretino wanted the wealthy Duke of Mantua as his patron, he did not send a weeping letter asking for money.
* Instead, Aretino sent the Duke a priceless painting by his close friend, the master **Titian**.
* **The Psychology:** Lowborn servants beg for money. Aristocrats exchange fine gifts. By giving a world-class masterpiece, Aretino established an unspoken equality: *"We are two men of supreme culture."* The Duke treated him like nobility and funded him lavishly.

### 2. The David & Goliath Tactic
* If you are small and unknown, do not waste time arguing with other small competitors in your mud-pit.
* Challenge the industry giant on a clear, principled, or technical issue.
* By stepping into the ring with the heavyweight champion, the audience automatically assumes you belong in the championship division.

### 3. Deliberate Physical Demeanor
* **Slow your cadence:** When kings speak, they never rush their words because they know nobody will interrupt them.
* **Eliminate fidgeting:** Keep your hands still, plant your feet, and hold comfortable, unbroken eye contact. Stillness radiates authority; rapid movement radiates anxiety.

### In Corporate Politics: The Promotion Meeting
* **The Amateur Mistake:** Enters the boss’s office, hunches shoulders, and says: *"I know budgets are tight right now, but I was wondering if maybe I could be considered for a small step up?"* The boss mentally shelves the request.
* **The Regal Move:** Sits upright, maintains calm eye contact: *"Over the last two quarters, my team stabilized our primary operational pipeline, adding $300k in efficiency. To align my leadership with the next expansion phase, we need to formalize my promotion to Director. Here is the operational roadmap for that transition."* The conversation begins from a baseline of absolute inevitability.

### In Tech Startups: Interacting with Elite VCs
* Founders often grovel before venture capital firms, answering every email within 30 seconds and jumping through ridiculous hoops.
* **The Royal Shift:** The top founders remember that **capital is a commodity; visionary execution is rare**. 
* They treat investors like prospective business partners undergoing an interview: *"We are selecting partners who can accelerate our distribution in Asia. Why is your firm the right fit for our board?"* Investors compete furiously to win over founders who project this level of sovereignty.

### In Social Media & Digital Presence (LinkedIn / X)
* Stop writing needy posts begging for engagement: *"Please like and retweet if you found this helpful!"* (This screams low-status desperation).
* Publish authoritative, clean, unapologetic insights. Let your ideas stand on their own feet without begging for digital breadcrumbs.

## 🛡️ The Reversal: The Danger of the Severed Head (King Charles I)

Can acting like a king ever destroy you? **Yes: when it turns into rigid arrogance during a revolution.**

```
                     ARE YOU CONFRONTING A MASS REVOLT?
                                     │
                 ┌───────────────────┴───────────────────┐
                 ▼                                       ▼
              [ YES ]                                 [ NO ]
                 │                                       │
     THE CHARLES I TRAP:                       Act like a king.
     Stiff arrogance will trigger              Radiate quiet dignity
     execution. Show humility,                 and unshakeable self-worth.
     reform, and sacrifice (Law 45).
```

### 1. The Head of King Charles I (England, 1649)
* In the 1640s, England was gripped by massive democratic protests and civil war led by Oliver Cromwell.
* **The Error:** Instead of reading the room and making strategic, flexible compromises, King Charles I doubled down on his "Divine Right of Kings." He disbanded Parliament, treated the citizens with icy, contemptuous arrogance, and refused to negotiate.
* **The Price:** The enraged populace put him on trial for high treason and publicly chopped his head off.
* **The Rule:** Regal bearing is for your **daily self-respect and standard operations**. If an overwhelming revolution or crisis erupts, clinging to stiff pride will get you guillotined. In extreme storms, bend like the reed (Law 22) or preach reform (Law 45).

### 2. The Clown Emperor (Caligula's Vulgarity)
* Roman Emperor Caligula thought royal status meant he could do whatever bizarre thing entered his head: receiving ambassadors in women's negligees, shouting obscenities, and electing his horse as consul.
* His vulgar mockery of Roman traditions disgusted the Praetorian Guard, who assassinated him in a palace corridor. Royal status requires **dignity and decorum**, never chaotic buffoonery.
