# Law 08: Make Other People Come to You—Use Bait If Necessary

> **Core Rule:** When you force other people to act on your terms, you control the game. It is always superior to make your opponent come to you, abandoning their plans, schedules, and comfort zones. Lure them with the promise of huge gain, let them exhaust their energy traveling to your ground, and then strike.

---

## 📌 Executive Summary (The Plain Truth)

Most people confuse **aggressive movement** with **real power**. They run around frantically trying to fix every problem, attack every critic, and chase after every client or deal. **This is an illusion of control.**

When you chase someone:
- You operate on their schedule and on their territory.
- You burn your precious mental and physical stamina.
- You become totally reactive, constantly responding to their moves instead of directing the board.

Real power is **magnetic, not frantic**:
- You sit back, stay calm, and set up an irresistible piece of bait (money, status, curiosity, or an apparent weakness).
- The opponent abandons their original plans and rushes toward your trap.
- By the time they reach you, they are exhausted, out of position, and completely under your control.

## 📜 Complete Historical Lessons (Broken Down Into Plain Logic)

### Lesson 1: The Trap That Ended Napoleon (Talleyrand & The Elba Escape)

* **The Exile (1814):** After Napoleon’s first defeat, European powers exiled him to the Mediterranean island of Elba. The major powers gathered in Vienna to divide up Europe. But everyone was terrified that Napoleon would return—he was still energetic, brilliant, and hungry for conquest.
* **Talleyrand’s Secret Scheme:** Talleyrand, Napoleon's former foreign minister, knew that an outright assassination would turn Napoleon into a permanent martyr and spark fresh civil war. Instead, Talleyrand decided to **bait Napoleon into destroying himself**.
* **The Bait:**
  - Talleyrand arranged for visitors to secretly whisper into Napoleon’s ear on Elba that the French people despised the new king and were praying for the Emperor's return.
  - Austrian agents convinced Napoleon that if he returned, the European powers would not stop him.
  - British naval ships guarding the island were deliberately ordered to pull back and look the other way in broad daylight.
* **The Snap of the Trap:** Napoleon swallowed the bait whole. Believing destiny was calling him, he escaped Elba with 900 men and marched on Paris. The French public temporarily welcomed him (the famous "Hundred Days"), but France was financially broke and militarily depleted.
* **The Fatal End:** Having been drawn out of his safe island base onto open ground, Napoleon marched his desperate army into the Battle of Waterloo, where his forces were decisively demolished. This time, his enemies took no chances: they exiled him to the barren, inescapable rock of Saint Helena in the middle of the South Atlantic Ocean.

> **Lesson:** Talleyrand did not waste his breath trying to convince European diplomats to punish Napoleon further. He simply laid a glittering trail of bait (the promise of glory and power) that lured the Emperor out of hiding straight into an inescapable meat grinder.

### Lesson 2: The 6,000-Mile Exhaustion March (The Battle of Tsushima, 1905)

* **The Standoff:** In 1905, Russia and Japan went to war. The Russian army possessed a massive, technologically formidable navy, while Japan’s fleet was smaller and younger.
* **The Lure:** The Japanese commander, Admiral Togo Heihachiro, knew that attacking the Russian fleet in European waters was suicide. Instead, he leaked false intelligence indicating that Japan’s ports were vulnerable and could be easily wiped out in one decisive strike.
* **The Journey:** The Russian Baltic Fleet took the bait. Because Britain blocked the Suez Canal, the Russian ships had to sail around the entire African continent (over 6,000 extra miles) through scorching heat, storms, and hostile waters.
* **The Result:** The Russian sailors spent seven months on continuous high alert, terrified of ambush. By the time they finally reached the Straits of Tsushima in Japan, the men were sick, exhausted, and demoralized, and their ship hulls were thick with sea growth. Admiral Togo’s fresh, well-rested fleet was waiting calmly in familiar home waters and obliterated the Russian navy in a matter of hours.

> **Lesson:** When you force your rival to cross vast distances to meet you, the journey itself does half the killing.

### Lesson 3: The Dropped Handkerchief on Wall Street (Daniel Drew)

* **The Master Speculator:** In 19th-century America, Daniel Drew was one of the most feared operators on Wall Street. He rarely approached buyers or sellers directly because he knew direct offers create suspicion and haggling.
* **The Bait:** When Drew wanted to artificially manipulate a stock price, he would walk casually through a private club frequented by elite stockbrokers. 
  - He would pretend to be in a tremendous hurry, mopping his forehead with a large red bandana.
  - As he pulled out the bandana, a tiny piece of folded paper would "accidentally" fall from his pocket onto the carpet. Drew would hurry out the door without noticing.
* **The Frenzy:** The club members would rush over, grab the scrap, and unfold it to find what looked like a confidential internal memo advising an immediate purchase or sale of a specific railway stock.
* **The Result:** The greedy brokers would rush to execute the trade in massive volumes, driving the stock exactly where Drew had planned, allowing him to dump his holdings at peak prices and pocket millions.

> **Lesson:** People value information that they believe they stole or found through their own cleverness ten times more than advice given to them openly.

### Lesson 4: The Strategic Illness (Filippo Brunelleschi)

* **The Commission:** Filippo Brunelleschi, the genius Renaissance architect, was commissioned to build the massive dome of the Santa Maria del Fiore cathedral in Florence.
* **The Obstacle:** City bureaucrats did not trust Brunelleschi alone, so they appointed a second architect, **Lorenzo Ghiberti**, as co-director. Ghiberti was politically connected, intended to do none of the difficult technical calculations, and planned to take half the credit and pay.
* **The Retreat:** Brunelleschi did not scream, complain, or threaten to quit. At a critical, dangerous turning point in the construction when complex stone arches had to be raised, Brunelleschi suddenly went home, wrapped himself in blankets, and declared he was deathly ill.
* **The Collapse:** The site managers turned to Ghiberti for instructions. Ghiberti was completely incompetent and had no idea how to proceed. Work ground to a halt; workers protested; the city officials panicked. They visited Brunelleschi’s bedside, begging him to return. Brunelleschi said: *"Why do you need me when you have the brilliant Ghiberti?"*
* **The Victory:** The desperate officials fired Ghiberti on the spot and granted Brunelleschi sole authority and a massive salary increase. Within 48 hours, Brunelleschi made a "miraculous" recovery and finished the dome alone.

> **Lesson:** When your authority is diluted by a partner, step back and let the operation fail under their watch. When they are forced to come begging to you, you dictate the terms of your return.

### Lesson 5: The Pickpocket Signpost

* **The Paradox:** In crowded train stations throughout Europe, local authorities placed large signs reading: **BEWARE OF PICKPOCKETS**.
* **The Human Reaction:** Every traveler who reads the sign immediately pats their jacket or trousers to check if their wallet is still there.
* **The Exploit:** Professional pickpockets love these signs. They don't even have to search the crowd; they simply stand near the sign and watch where everyone puts their hands.

> **Lesson:** A simple suggestion or small trigger will force people to reveal their most guarded secrets and vulnerabilities without them realizing they did so.

### 1. The Home-Ground Advantage (Negotiation Setup)
* **The Rule:** Never negotiate high-stakes deals in the other party's boardroom or office if you can avoid it.
* **Execution:** Host meetings at your headquarters, a private venue you control, or a neutral territory of your choosing. On your home turf, you control the temperature, the seating, the tech, the timing, and the exits. The guest is subconsciously on the defensive.

### 2. The Lure of the "Easy Win"
* When dealing with an arrogant or suspicious competitor, **feign weakness or laziness**.
* Leave an apparent gap in your pricing, marketing, or defense. Let them think they have discovered an easy angle to exploit you.
* Once they overcommit their capital or resources to pursue that opening, spring your trap and cut off their exit.

### 3. The Sudden Withdrawal (Brunelleschi’s Playbook)
* If a client, partner, or employer is undervaluing your work or treating you like a replaceable cog:
  - Do not argue.
  - Step away completely during a high-stakes, critical moment (take a vacation, claim sickness, or refuse to sign a contract until a deadline).
  - Let them experience 72 hours of reality *without* your presence.
  - When they feel the walls closing in, they will come to you, ready to pay whatever you demand.

## 🌐 Modern Workplace & Digital Scenarios

### In Enterprise Sales & Consulting: Inbound vs. Outbound
* **The Chaser:** An agency founder sends 50 cold messages a day on LinkedIn begging for a 15-minute discovery call: *"Please give me a chance to show our deck!"* Result: Zero respect, viewed as spam, treated like a commodity.
* **The Baiter:** The founder publishes two ruthless, high-value technical tear-downs of an industry vulnerability, showing how enterprise companies are losing $200k/month. 
  - They offer a free, exclusive diagnostic tool via link.
  - Enterprise VPs sign up and email the founder directly: *"Can your team audit our systems this month?"*
  - The founder sets the price and the terms because the client came to them.

### In Tech Product Growth: Artificial Scarcity & Waitlists
* When launching new software, never make it wide-open to everyone on Day 1.
* **The Tactic:** Launch with an invite-only waitlist (e.g., early Gmail, Clubhouse, Superhuman). 
* When you tell people they cannot have access immediately, their desire multiplies. They share links, beg existing users for invites, and market your product for free just to be let inside the club.

### In Cybersecurity: The Honeypot Architecture
* High-tier cyber defense does not chase hackers across the internet.
* Defense engineers deploy **Honeypots**—decoy servers containing fake credential stores, simulated databases, and enticing filenames (e.g., `prod_db_backup_2024.sql`).
* The attacker spots the easy target, enters the environment, and begins exfiltrating the data. While the attacker believes they have breached the crown jewels, the defenders silently isolate the IP, study the adversary’s tradecraft, and patch the real network without the attacker ever knowing they were captured.

### The Blitzkrieg Exception (Cesare Borgia & Napoleon)
When time is running against you, or when your opponent is small, disorganized, and vulnerable, **waiting gives them time to regroup and find allies**.
- **The Execution:** Do not wait or bait; use overwhelming speed. Strike like lightning before they can blink.
- As boxing champion Joe Louis famously said: *"He can run, but he can't hide."*
- Speed paralyzes the opponent's nervous system. If an enemy has no time to think, they make catastrophic mistakes.
